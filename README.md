# Multiplex Assays of Variant Effects (MAVEs): Computational Analysis of Genetic Variant Function

## 1. Introduction

Multiplex Assays of Variant Effects (MAVEs) are high-throughput experimental approaches designed to measure the functional consequences of thousands of genetic variants simultaneously.

In these experiments, researchers construct libraries containing thousands of mutated versions of a gene, introduce these variants into cells, and measure how individual mutations affect gene function.

Depending on the experimental design, MAVEs can be used to investigate changes in protein activity, gene expression, RNA splicing, protein stability, and molecular interactions.

These approaches provide valuable insights into the relationship between genetic variation, molecular function, and disease mechanisms.

## 2. Research Background and Applications

MAVEs have broad applications in biomedical research and genetic engineering.

Major research areas include:

- **Disease Genetics:** Investigating functional effects of genetic variants in disease-associated genes, including genes involved in insulin resistance, Parkinson's disease, and rare developmental disorders.
- **Functional Genomics:** Understanding how genetic variations influence protein function and cellular processes.
- **Gene Expression:** Studying how mutations affect transcriptional activity and gene expression.
- **RNA Splicing:** Investigating how genetic variants alter pre-mRNA splicing and transcript processing.
- **Genetic Engineering:** Identifying sequence modifications that influence gene expression, protein function, and other biological properties.

## 3. Project Objectives

The primary objective of this project is to develop a computational workflow for processing, analyzing, and interpreting data generated by Multiplex Assays of Variant Effects.

Specific objectives include:

1. Retrieving publicly available MAVE datasets from scientific repositories.
2. Performing data preprocessing and quality control.
3. Characterizing genetic variants at nucleotide and protein levels.
4. Quantifying and comparing variant-specific functional effects.
5. Identifying variants associated with altered biological function.
6. Investigating relationships between genetic variation and functional outcomes.
7. Comparing results across different experimental assays.
8. Visualizing functional effects and identifying biologically relevant patterns.
9. Interpreting computational findings in the context of functional genomics and disease biology.

## 5. Methodology and Computational Workflow

The project follows a step-by-step computational workflow.

### Step 1: Data Acquisition

- Identify suitable publicly available MAVE datasets.
- Retrieve experimental variant-effect measurements.
- Extract variant identifiers, nucleotide and protein changes, functional scores, and experimental metadata.
- Document dataset accessions and original publications.

### Step 2: Data Preprocessing and Quality Control

- Inspect dataset structure and data types.
- Identify missing values and duplicated records.
- Evaluate data quality and experimental coverage.
- Handle incomplete observations.
- Prepare datasets for downstream analysis.

### Step 3: Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

- Examine functional score distributions.
- Calculate descriptive statistics.
- Investigate variation in functional measurements.
- Identify potential outliers.
- Explore relationships between variant characteristics and functional scores.

### Step 4: Variant Annotation

- Characterize nucleotide-level substitutions.
- Identify amino acid changes where applicable.
- Classify variants according to their molecular consequences.
- Investigate relationships between mutation characteristics and functional effects.

### Step 5: Statistical Analysis

- Calculate summary statistics for functional measurements.
- Compare functional scores across variant groups.
- Apply appropriate statistical tests.
- Evaluate correlations between experimental measurements.
- Assess measurement uncertainty and statistical significance.

### Step 6: Functional Impact Assessment

- Identify variants associated with altered biological activity.
- Investigate variants with potentially disruptive functional effects.
- Compare measurements with appropriate reference and control variants.
- Explore relationships between variant effects and biological mechanisms.

### Step 7: Comparative Analysis

- Compare results across independent experimental assays.
- Investigate similarities and differences in variant-effect profiles.
- Identify mutations with assay-dependent functional consequences.

### Step 8: Data Visualization

Generate scientific visualizations, including:

- Functional score distribution plots.
- Variant-effect comparison plots.
- Mutation heatmaps.
- Correlation plots.
- Position-specific functional profiles.

### Step 9: Biological Interpretation

- Interpret functional effects in the context of gene and protein biology.
- Investigate potential disease-related implications.
- Discuss experimental and computational limitations.
- Relate findings to published scientific literature.

## 7. Case Study: BRCA1 Functional Variant Analysis

As an initial practical application, this project includes a case study investigating functional variants in the BRCA1 gene using publicly available Deep Mutational Scanning (DMS) datasets.

The analysis focuses on two complementary experimental approaches:

- **E3 Ubiquitin Ligase Assay:** Investigating the effects of genetic variants on BRCA1-associated E3 ubiquitin ligase activity.
- **Yeast Two-Hybrid (Y2H) Assay:** Investigating the effects of genetic variants on protein–protein interactions.

### Research Questions

1. Which BRCA1 variants are associated with altered functional activity?
2. How do different amino acid substitutions influence protein function?
3. Are functional effects consistent across the two experimental assays?
4. Which variants demonstrate assay-dependent functional consequences?

### Data Sources

- BRCA1 E3 assay: `urn:mavedb:00000003-a-1`
- BRCA1 Y2H assay: `urn:mavedb:00000003-b-1`
